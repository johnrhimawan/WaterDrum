WaterDrum: Watermarking
for Data-centric Unlearning

Existing utility-centric unlearning metrics break down precisely where they matter most — when the forget and retain sets look alike, when retraining from scratch is impractical, and when the model owner can game the metric. WaterDrum moves the evidence of unlearning into the data itself, by stamping each owner's content with a robust text watermark that the unlearned model either still emits, or no longer can.

TL;DR

WaterDrum is the first data-centric unlearning metric for LLMs. Each data owner's training text is stamped with a robust Waterfall watermark before fine-tuning; after unlearning, we verify whether the watermark still survives in the model's outputs for that owner's prompts. The result is a metric that is separable across forget/retain sets, linearly calibrated to how much was unlearned, feasible without a retrained reference model, and — uniquely — robust to exact and semantic duplicates, where every utility-centric baseline collapses to chance.

The Problem

The Quiet Failure of Utility-Centric Metrics

Machine unlearning has become a frontline requirement for deploying large language models: regulators, content owners, and individual users increasingly need the influence of specific training data removed from a deployed model. The question is no longer just whether we can unlearn, but whether we can credibly measure that we have.

For years, that measurement has been utility-centric — we compare the unlearned model to a hypothetical "retrained-from-scratch" oracle on metrics like ROUGE, Truth Ratio, KnowMem, or membership-inference scores. This works in toy settings. In realistic ones, it breaks in three quiet ways:

a
Semantically similar forget & retain sets
Two news outlets paraphrase the same press release; a textbook restates a Wikipedia paragraph. A metric that scores output quality cannot separate "removed this owner's text" from "still knows the same facts from another owner." Under exact duplicates, baselines such as ROUGE and Truth Ratio collapse to chance-level AUROC (~0.5) — they literally cannot tell forget from retain.
b
No retrained reference model
"Just retrain without the forget set" is the standard sanity check, and it is computationally absurd at frontier scale. Without that oracle, utility-centric metrics have no anchor — they produce a number, but no notion of where "perfectly unlearned" should land.
c
Model owners can game the metric
If the metric is a public output statistic on a small probe set, a model owner can satisfy it without performing any unlearning at all — by post-hoc filtering, refusal training, or output rewriting. The metric measures behavior, not influence.
Each of these failures shares the same root cause: utility-centric metrics ask the model what it knows. WaterDrum asks the data whether it is still being used.
What an Unlearning Metric Should Do

Four Desiderata, One Yardstick

Before proposing WaterDrum, the paper crystallises what we actually want from an unlearning metric. The four desiderata are not aspirational — they are the diagnostic test that every metric in the literature is then graded against.

D1
Separability
The metric must distinguish whether a given owner's data still influences the model. Concretely, retain-set queries should produce higher metric values than forget-set queries — operationalised as AUROC ≈ 1 between the two distributions.
D2
Calibration
The aggregate metric should scale proportionally with the fraction of forget data still effectively present. This turns unlearning from a binary verdict into a continuous, auditable measurement — and lets us detect partial unlearning.
D3
Feasibility
No retrained-from-scratch reference model. Query-only access to the LLM — no weights, no logits, no internal probes. The metric should be computable in the regime where real deployments actually live.
D4
Robustness to Similar Data
D1 and D2 should continue to hold when the forget and retain sets contain semantically similar or even exactly duplicated content. This is where every utility-centric metric we tested fails — and where data-centric watermarking quietly thrives.
Read across the four: D1 is the binary test, D2 is the continuous test, D3 is the deployability test, D4 is the realism test. A metric that passes three but fails one is not enough.
Method

Watermark the Data, Not the Model

The architectural shift is small to state and large in consequence. Instead of measuring what the model produces on a probe set, we modify the training data itself before fine-tuning, so that each data owner's content carries a hidden, owner-specific signature. After unlearning, we ask a simple question of the model's outputs: is owner i's signature still there?

A Three-Stage Pipeline

1
Watermark each owner's text (Waterfall)
Each data owner i applies the Waterfall text watermarking framework with a private key μᵢ. Waterfall paraphrases the owner's documents while statistically biasing token choices in a key-dependent way: a verifier with the same key can later detect the watermark's presence in any text derived from those documents, including LLM outputs that simply learned from them.
2
Fine-tune on the watermarked corpus
The LLM is fine-tuned as usual on the watermarked dataset. Because each owner contributes only watermarked text, the model implicitly learns each owner's statistical signature — but no document IDs, prompts, or special tokens are exposed. The watermark is invisible to downstream users and to the model itself.
3
Verify after unlearning
For each owner i, sample model continuations φ(qd) from prompts drawn from that owner's domain. Apply the Waterfall verifier 𝒱(·, μᵢ): a watermark-strength score that is high when owner i's influence persists, and near zero when it has been unlearned. No retrained reference. No model internals.

The Metric in One Line

For an LLM φ, a prompt distribution over owner i's domain, and that owner's watermark key μᵢ, the per-query WaterDrum metric is simply:

Metric Definition 1 
  M'(φ(q_d), i) := 𝒱(φ(q_d), μ_i)

  // 𝒱 is the Waterfall verifier;
  // μ_i is owner i's private watermark key;
  // q_d is a prompt drawn from owner i's domain;
  // φ(q_d) is the LLM's continuation.

Aggregating M′ across many prompts gives a per-owner watermark strength. Comparing forget-set owners against retain-set owners yields the AUROC for D1; comparing aggregate strength against the fraction of remaining forget data yields the calibration curve for D2.

The signal is in the data, not in the metric's framing. Even if a model owner rewrites outputs, refuses queries, or filters generations, the watermark either still survives in some continuations or it doesn't — and the verifier can tell.

Results

What Survives the Duplicate Stress Test

We grade every metric — ROUGE, Truth Ratio, KnowMem, MIA, and WaterDrum — against the four desiderata across three data regimes: No Duplicate (the easy case the literature has lived in), Semantic Duplicate (paraphrased overlap with similarity ≈ 0.94), and Exact Duplicate (copies of forget content also appearing in the retain set).

Calibration (D2) · R²

Calibration asks whether the metric scales linearly with how much forget data remains. The slope, intercept, and noise are summarised by R². Only WaterDrum keeps R² ≈ 1 across all three regimes; the others swing wildly negative, meaning their "calibration curves" are flat or non-monotonic — useless for partial-unlearning audits.

The asymmetry is structural, not anecdotal. ROUGE asks "does the model still produce text that looks like the forget set?" — and under exact duplicates, every retain-set query also looks like the forget set. WaterDrum asks "does the model still produce text that carries owner i's watermark key μᵢ?" — and that question has the same answer regardless of how the surface text overlaps. The watermark survives paraphrase by design.

Across unlearning algorithms (gradient ascent, NPO, DPO-based unlearning, and full retraining), WaterDrum tracks the actual influence of the forget set rather than its linguistic shadow — and is the only metric whose ordering of algorithms agrees with the retrained-from-scratch oracle across all duplicate regimes.

Key Insight

One Thing to Remember

💧
Utility-centric metrics ask the model what it still produces. WaterDrum stamps the data with a per-owner watermark, so the metric becomes a question about the data itself — separable, calibrated, and feasible even when forget and retain sets are word-for-word identical.

For practitioners deploying unlearning in regulated environments, the practical handle is small and concrete: watermark the corpus once with Waterfall before fine-tuning, keep each owner's key privately, and run the verifier on a fixed prompt set after any unlearning operation. The metric is reproducible, monotone in the amount of unlearning, and resistant to the kinds of corner-cases that have historically made unlearning audits a soft science.

For researchers, the bigger handle is the framing: treat the data as the unit of measurement, not the model. The four desiderata, the two benchmarks, and the public verifier are now the floor for evaluating any future unlearning algorithm.

Limitations & Future Work

Where WaterDrum Doesn't Reach (Yet)

WaterDrum's strength is exactly its scope. The metric is designed to evaluate unlearning under fine-tuning regimes where the data owner controls how their content is ingested. Four honest caveats follow from that:

Pre-Watermarked Training Data

Owners must apply Waterfall before their content enters the training corpus. For unlearning on already-pretrained models with unwatermarked data, WaterDrum cannot retroactively recover a signature; a hybrid with watermark-free baselines is needed.

Watermark Adversaries

A determined adversary that aggressively paraphrases or denoises model outputs may degrade the watermark verifier's signal. Waterfall is robust to standard paraphrase, but adaptive removal attacks remain an open frontier.

Per-Owner Key Management

Each owner needs a private key μᵢ at watermarking time and at verification time. Key custody, rotation, and revocation are real operational concerns the paper acknowledges but does not solve.

Modality & Domain Scope

Evaluation is on QA (TOFU) and long-form text (arXiv abstracts) in English. Code, structured data, multilingual corpora, and multimodal settings are not yet covered, and the watermarking primitive will need domain-specific extensions.

Future directions: tighter integration with continual-unlearning algorithms, watermarking schemes whose verifier strength depends on the quantity of an owner's data rather than just its presence, and adversarial robustness benchmarks for paraphrase-based watermark removal.
